\subsection{Matched Event-Contribution Comparison}
\label{sec:event-contribution}
The checkpoint-backed V81 single-modality replication shows that event-only detection is the weakest standalone stream, with $0.1949\pm0.0012$ COCO AP, compared with $0.4473\pm0.0033$ for RGB-only and $0.5196\pm0.0196$ for thermal-only. Weak standalone performance does not, however, imply that the event stream is useless when fused with complementary modalities. V86 therefore isolates the event input under a matched dynamic-gating architecture, training schedule, component-disjoint development-validation split, evaluator, checkpoint rule, and seed set.

The RGB+thermal dynamic model reaches $0.6912\pm0.0280$ AP, $0.9461\pm0.0028$ AP50, $0.8409\pm0.0241$ AP75, and $0.7673\pm0.0232$ AR100. The matched RGB+thermal+event dynamic model reaches $0.7251\pm0.0121$ AP, $0.9475\pm0.0003$ AP50, $0.8742\pm0.0081$ AP75, and $0.7917\pm0.0098$ AR100 (\tabref{tab:event-contribution-v86}). The same-seed tri-modal minus two-modal AP differences are $-0.0110$, $+0.0657$, and $+0.0471$ for seeds 0, 1, and 2, respectively, giving a mean paired difference of $+0.0339\pm0.0400$ with positive gains in two of three seeds. The mean paired differences are $+0.0015$ for AP50, $+0.0333$ for AP75, and $+0.0244$ for AR100, each positive in two of three seeds.

The event-associated mean gain is therefore concentrated in stricter localization and recall rather than the lenient AP50 operating point. The evidence supports a complementary event contribution on average under this frozen development-validation protocol, but it does not support uniform per-seed improvement, statistical significance, universal event-sensor utility, or a physical sensor-health interpretation.
